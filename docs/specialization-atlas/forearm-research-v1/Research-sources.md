# Research sources and interpretation

Read alongside START-HERE-Claude.md. All exercise scores are heuristic; no cited paper validates the entire score matrix. Source references are support for anatomy, specific acute findings or general training principles, not proof of every mapped coefficient.

Publication: University anatomy reference
Evidence access: retrieved page text

Anatomical actions; cross-check attachment details with A02.

## A02 — Loyola upper extremity muscle table
Source: https://www.stritch.luc.edu/lumen/meded/grossanatomy/homepage/muscletables/upperextremitymuscletable.pdf
Publication: University anatomy reference
Evidence access: retrieved PDF text

Anatomical actions and attachments. Not exercise effect percentages.

## S02 — Effects of forearm rotation on wrist flexor and extensor muscle activities.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39819577/
Publication: 2025 Jan 17
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

At controlled torque: ECRB higher with pronation; FCR higher with supination; FCU lower pronated than neutral; ECU not different. Analog for wrist variants only.

## S03 — Electromyographic activity and strength during maximum isometric pronation and supination efforts in healthy adults.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14656682/
Publication: 2004 Jan
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Fine-wire pronation/supination: PT/PQ pronate, supinator/biceps supinate; deep PQ may stabilize in both. Not isolation percentages.

## S04 — Characterizing forearm muscle activity in university-aged males during dynamic radial-ulnar deviation of the wrist using a wrist robot.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32636008/
Publication: 2020 Jul 17
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Robot-based dynamic deviation experiment: posture, direction and phase affect activation/co-contraction. Not dumbbell-specific allocation.

## S05 — The effects of isometric hand grip force on wrist kinematics and forearm muscle activity during radial and ulnar wrist joint perturbations.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35646483/
Publication: 2022
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Increasing grip force increased most recorded forearm activity, including wrist extensors, during perturbations. Not carry/hang percentage calibration.

## S06 — Muscular coordination of biceps brachii and brachioradialis in elbow flexion with respect to hand position.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26300781/
Publication: 2015
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Elbow flexion hand position changes BR activation; small acute study. No long-term hypertrophy ranking.

## S07 — Impact of Fat Grip Attachments on Muscular Strength and Neuromuscular Activation During Resistance Exercise.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30694963/
Publication: 2021 Feb 01
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Thick grips reduced strength performance and increased forearm EMG in some tested lifts, not all. No universal thick-grip multiplier.

## S08 — Selective recruitment of single motor units in human flexor digitorum superficialis muscle during flexion of individual fingers.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15946972/
Publication: 2005 Aug 15
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

FDS motor units show selective recruitment across fingers. Whole-muscle grip allocation cannot resolve all digits.

## S09 — Neither load nor systemic hormones determine resistance training-mediated hypertrophy or strength gains in resistance-trained young men.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27174923/
Publication: 2016 Jul 01
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Trained-men trial: 20–25 versus 8–12 reps to failure produced similar hypertrophy outcomes; not isolated forearm-muscle data.

## S10 — Effects of different intensities of resistance training with equated volume load on muscle strength and hypertrophy.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29564973/
Publication: 2018 Jul
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Trial comparing 20/40/60/80% 1RM: very low load underperformed, higher loads favored strength. Not precise forearm rep optima.

## S11 — Cross-education after high-frequency versus low-frequency volume-matched handgrip training.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28249351/
Publication: 2017 Oct
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Small four-week isometric handgrip study: volume-matched frequencies both improved grip; forearm thickness changes do not identify each muscle response.

## S12 — The Resistance Training Dose Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gains.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/
Publication: 2026 Feb
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

67-study meta-regression: distinguishing direct and indirect sets helped fit; diminishing returns. Fractional 0.5 is not validated for each small forearm muscle.

## S13 — Exploring the Dose-Response Relationship Between Estimated Resistance Training Proximity to Failure, Strength Gain, and Muscle Hypertrophy: A Series of Meta-Regressions.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/
Publication: 2024 Sep
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Exploratory meta-regressions associate closer failure with more hypertrophy; exact relationship uncertain. Do not compute precise effective-rep bonuses.

## S14 — Isometric training and long-term adaptations: Effects of muscle length, intensity, and intent: A systematic review.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30580468/
Publication: 2019 Apr
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Review: isometric adaptations depend on length, intensity and intent. No universal seconds-to-dynamic-set conversion.

## S15 — Mechanical, Material and Morphological Adaptations of Healthy Lower Limb Tendons to Mechanical Loading: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35657492/
Publication: 2022 Oct
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Loading adapts tendon properties in lower-limb evidence. Not forearm injury thresholds; force/strain cannot be inferred from logged kg alone.

## S16 — The association between combination of hand force and forearm posture and incidence of lateral epicondylitis in a working population.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24669550/
Publication: 2014 Feb
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Occupational prospective study links combinations of force and posture with lateral epicondylitis. Workshift hazard ratios cannot become gym injury probabilities.

## S17 — Quantitative Measures of Physical Risk Factors Associated with Work-Related Musculoskeletal Disorders of the Elbow: A Systematic Review.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30621312/
Publication: 2019 Jan 05
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Review of work-related elbow risk factors. Population/exposure context limits transfer to training.

## S18 — Strength and Hypertrophy Adaptations Between Low- vs. High-Load Resistance Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/
Publication: 2017 Dec
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Meta-analysis: higher loads favor 1RM strength; hypertrophy occurs across loading ranges in studied conditions. Not a muscle-specific optimal rep table.

## S19 — Wrist tendon forces during various dynamic wrist motions.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20353863/
Publication: 2010 Apr
Evidence access: PubMed abstract and metadata; not a full-text systematic review

Cadaver wrist simulator: tendon forces vary by movement. Not live exercise injury probabilities or hypertrophy weights.
