# Specialization atlas: hip anatomy and its evidence (19 Oct 2026)

What the hips' anatomy content would say, what it rests on, and how far that review can be trusted. This is the anatomy lane's research dossier for wave E; the content files it feeds are `shared/specialization/regions/hips/{nodes,nodeActions,claims}.ts` and `shared/specialization/sources/hips.ts`. Ids, names, short names, kinds, roles, layers, parents and region actions are those fixed in 10-multi-region.md §1.2; the method, limits and wording follow 03-anatomy-evidence.md. The machine-readable form, with every query and the excerpt relied on for each citation, is `dossier.json` beside this file; the raw search log is `searchlog.md`. Nothing here reaches the app or the database until the hips' anatomy lane has turned it into content and that has passed its own adversarial review.

**In short.** 13 nodes, 33 claims (13 function, 13 appearance, 5 selectivity, 2 variation), all 33 reviewed and none draft; 24 node actions (12 prime, 12 assist); no new joint action (all six hip actions are already in `shared/specialization/actions/joints.ts`); 59 sources, every one cited by a claim; 90 citations, each with its query and excerpt; 2 deviations from §1.2, neither of which changes an id. Two product questions are open (§6): the tensor fasciae latae has no main action on the evidence, so the content test 'gives every muscle a main action' will fail for it, and no hip muscle is a main mover of inward rotation.

## 1. Method

**What was reviewed.** Every node of the hips in §1.2: the gluteal muscles (a parent) and the gluteus maximus, medius and minimus; the tensor fasciae latae; the piriformis (adjacent); the iliopsoas; the adductor group (a parent) and the adductor magnus, longus and brevis; the pectineus and gracilis (adjacent). For each, a function and an appearance claim, plus selectivity claims where a citable EMG finding was found (five) and variation claims where a real difference between people was found (two). The external members, the rectus femoris and the three hamstrings, belong to the thigh lane and are not researched here.

**How.** Web search only, as for the forearm. Page fetches are refused by the network proxy, so each claim was checked against what the search results showed: titles, URLs and the tool's generated summaries with quoted excerpts. 58 searches were run (Q1 to Q58 in `searchlog.md`), batched by muscle and by question. For each claim:

1. Search for reputable references in the evidence standard's order: NCBI Bookshelf (StatPearls), peer-reviewed papers, university and teaching pages (TeachMeAnatomy, the Stanford Bassett collection, a University of British Columbia dissection image, the 1918 Gray's Anatomy), Kenhub, Elsevier Complete Anatomy, Radiopaedia, IMAIOS. Wikipedia, WikiMSK and Physiopedia appeared in results and were read; none is cited.
2. Add each relied-on reference with a `<publisher>.<topic>` key, or `pmid.<number>` where the PubMed id was shown, and a note saying exactly what the search showed and what it could not confirm.
3. Cite it on the claim with a locator (the section the excerpt came from) and a qualification where the source is limited (search excerpt or summary, location only, abstract only, acute EMG, a video transcript, image labels).
4. Record, for every citation, the query and the excerpt relied on, verbatim from the search output (`citations` in the JSON; fragments of one result are joined with ' ... '). A script checked that every excerpt fragment appears word for word in `searchlog.md`, and that every citation's query is a logged query.
5. Mark a claim `reviewed` only when the recorded excerpts support every sentence, with `reviewedAt: "2026-10-19"` and the note "Checked against the cited sources through web search on 19 Oct 2026; automated review, not a clinician or anatomist." Appearance claims carry the forearm's appearance note adapted to the date and are always `anatomical_inference`.
6. A node is `reviewed` only when all its function claims are. Parents (the gluteals and the adductors) have a function claim only because the references speak of each group as such (TeachMeAnatomy and Kenhub on the gluteal muscles; Kenhub and StatPearls on the hip adductors); they have no node actions.

Wording was rewritten until each sentence said no more than its excerpts. Where references disagree (rotation by the adductors and the iliopsoas, the tensor fasciae latae at the knee, the piriformis's turning point), the claim says that they disagree and the action is left out of the node actions. Where an action changes with hip flexion and the evidence says so (the piriformis, the gluteus minimus, the adductor longus), the claim says so plainly rather than picking one. A build script also checked the copy rules of `regionContent.test.ts` and `forearmAnatomy.test.ts` over every node field, statement, locator and qualification: no per-cent sign, no growth promise, no 'optimal' or 'guarantee', no vendor name, no 'table', no screen left or right; deep target muscles say 'little or no' contour; surface target muscles' shape notes are qualified.

## 2. Limits

- **Excerpts and summaries, not full texts.** StatPearls showed introductions and parts of Structure and Function; several articles were behind a registration wall past that. Many results came as generated summaries with a few quoted lines, and the Kenhub videos as transcript summaries. Each citation's qualification says which.
- **Search summaries can misquote.** Function claims rest on two to five references each. Lines a summary did not tie to a named page were not used (for example a line calling the adductor longus 'the most anterior and superficial of the adductor muscles', Q52, and a Radiopaedia function sentence, Q54). Some figures came only from secondary summaries of an abstract (the 2013 gluteal-versus-TFL EMG study), and the claim's qualification says so.
- **Image labels.** The adductor magnus's back-view outcrop rests on the labels of two posterior dissection images described by a search summary; the images were not seen.
- **Automated review.** "Reviewed" here means checked by this automated process against search results. It is not a review by a clinician or an anatomist, and the database lane should not publish it as clinically verified on the strength of it.
- **Nothing here is hypertrophy evidence.** The five selectivity claims cite acute EMG studies. They say what was measured and that it is not growth, and they must never become set-credit weights. No longitudinal growth study is cited.

Said plainly, as §8 requires on every region's "How this is counted":

> "Reviewed" means an automated agent checked each sentence against web-search excerpts and generated summaries of the cited sources on the date shown. It was not checked against the full texts, and not by a clinician or anatomist. A second automated review, with the same tools and limits, looked for errors; it is not peer review. The drawings are original schematics checked against these notes, not reviewed by an anatomist.

(No second review has been run on this dossier yet.)

### The claims, in full

**Gluteal muscles** (`hips.gluteals`)

- `hips.gluteals.function` (function, established_anatomy, reviewed): The gluteal muscles of the buttock are taught in two groups. A superficial group of large muscles, the gluteus maximus, medius and minimus with the tensor fasciae latae, extends the hip and moves the thigh out to the side; a deep group of smaller muscles, the piriformis among them, mainly turns the thigh outward. The gluteus medius and minimus also hold the pelvis steady when one foot is off the ground, as in walking.

**Gluteus maximus** (`hips.gluteus_maximus`)

- `hips.gluteus_maximus.function` (function, established_anatomy, reviewed): The gluteus maximus extends the hip and turns the thigh outward (external rotation). Its upper fibres help move the thigh out to the side and its lower fibres help bring it in. Anatomy references describe it as the main hip extensor when force is needed, as in rising from a chair, straightening up from a bend, climbing and running. Of the hip muscles measured in a cadaver study, it had the largest capacity for turning the thigh outward. Through the iliotibial band it helps support the straightened knee.
  - statpearls.gluteus_maximus, Introduction; Structure and Function. Qualification: Seen through a generated web-search summary of the page, not the page itself.
  - kenhub.gluteus_maximus, Key facts: Action. Qualification: Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
  - teachmeanatomy.gluteal_region, Superficial muscles: gluteus maximus. Qualification: Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
  - pmid.10327003, Abstract. Qualification: Cadaver and computer-model study of rotation leverage (moment arms) in four specimens; abstract only.
  - imaios.gluteus_maximus, Function. Qualification: Seen through a web-search summary of the imaios.cn mirror, not the page.
- `hips.gluteus_maximus.selectivity.1` (selectivity, experimental, reviewed): In a fine-wire EMG study of 20 healthy adults doing 11 common therapeutic exercises, the upper part of the gluteus maximus was more active, relative to its own maximum, than the lower part in the five exercises that included moving the thigh out to the side or turning it outward. In the other six the two parts did not differ, and exercises that mainly extended the hip worked both parts to a similar extent. This is short-term muscle activity, not growth; the atlas counts the gluteus maximus as one muscle.
  - pmid.27494053, Abstract: Methods and Results. Qualification: Short-term muscle activity in a controlled task; not a measurement of growth and not a set-credit weight. Fine-wire electrodes in healthy adults; abstract only.
- `hips.gluteus_maximus.appearance` (appearance, anatomical_inference, reviewed): The gluteus maximus is the broad, thick muscle at the surface of the buttock and forms its prominence, so it may contribute most of the buttock's muscular outline. Visible shape also depends on neighbouring muscles, body fat and individual anatomy.
  - grays.gluteal_region, Gluteus maximus: description. Qualification: Gives location and layer only; the contour statement is inferred from them, not reported by the source.
  - kenhub.gluteal_muscles_video, Gluteus maximus: the superficial gluteal surface. Qualification: Gives location and layer only; the contour statement is inferred from them, not reported by the source. A video transcript seen through a search summary; the video was not watched.

**Gluteus medius** (`hips.gluteus_medius`)

- `hips.gluteus_medius.function` (function, established_anatomy, reviewed): The gluteus medius is a main hip abductor: the whole muscle moves the thigh out to the side, assisted by the gluteus minimus and the tensor fasciae latae. When one foot is off the ground, in standing, walking or running, it holds the pelvis steady over the standing leg. Its front fibres help turn the thigh inward, having the better leverage for it; one reference adds that its back fibres help turn the thigh outward. How hip muscles turn the thigh can change as the hip bends: a cadaver study of the gluteal muscles and the other hip rotators found the pull shifting toward turning the thigh inward with hip flexion in most of the muscle parts it measured.
  - statpearls.gluteus_medius, Structure and Function. Qualification: Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
  - kenhub.gluteus_medius, Function. Qualification: Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
  - kenhub.gluteus_medius_video, Function: inward turning by the front fibres. Qualification: A video transcript seen through a search summary; the video was not watched.
  - pmid.10327003, Abstract. Qualification: Cadaver and computer-model study of four specimens; the abstract does not say which parts of each muscle changed.
- `hips.gluteus_medius.selectivity.1` (selectivity, experimental, reviewed): In a fine-wire EMG study of 20 healthy adults doing 11 exercises, the gluteus medius and the upper gluteus maximus were both more active than the tensor fasciae latae in bridging on one or both legs, hip extension on hands and knees, the clam, side-stepping and squatting. The clam favoured the two gluteal muscles over the tensor fasciae latae most, followed by side-stepping, the one-leg bridge and the hands-and-knees exercises. This is short-term muscle activity, not growth, and it does not show which exercise builds which muscle.
  - pmid.23160432, Abstract: Results and Conclusions. Qualification: Short-term muscle activity in a controlled task; not a measurement of growth and not a set-credit weight. Healthy adults; the figures come from secondary summaries of the abstract.
- `hips.gluteus_medius.appearance` (appearance, anatomical_inference, reviewed): Only the back third of the gluteus medius lies under the gluteus maximus. Its larger front part, on the outer side of the hip, is covered by a thick sheet of fascia (the gluteal aponeurosis) rather than by muscle. It may therefore contribute to the contour of the upper outer hip beside the gluteus maximus, while its back third adds only to the fullness beneath that muscle. Visible shape also depends on neighbouring muscles, body fat and individual anatomy.
  - kenhub.gluteus_medius, Overview: what covers it. Qualification: Gives location and layer only; the contour statement is inferred from them, not reported by the source.
  - elsevier.gluteal_fascia, Description: the gluteal aponeurosis. Qualification: Gives location and layer only; the contour statement is inferred from them, not reported by the source.
  - imaios.gluteal_aponeurosis, Definition. Qualification: Gives location and layer only; the contour statement is inferred from them, not reported by the source. Seen through a web-search summary of the imaios.cn mirror, not the page.

**Gluteus minimus** (`hips.gluteus_minimus`)

- `hips.gluteus_minimus.function` (function, established_anatomy, reviewed): The gluteus minimus, the smallest of the gluteal muscles, lies just under the gluteus medius and is mainly a hip abductor and stabiliser: with the gluteus medius it moves the thigh out to the side and helps hold the pelvis steady in walking. Its front fibres help turn the thigh inward; one reference says this applies with the hip bent. In a cadaver and model study it could also act as a hip flexor, or turn the thigh either way, depending on the thigh's position and which part was working, and by tightening the hip joint capsule it helps hold the head of the thigh bone in its socket.
  - statpearls.gluteus_minimus, Introduction. Qualification: Introduction only; the rest of the article was behind a registration wall.
  - kenhub.gluteus_minimus, Function. Qualification: Seen through a generated web-search summary of the page, not the page itself.
  - kenhub.gluteus_minimus_video, Function: inward turning by the front fibres. Qualification: A video transcript seen through a search summary; the video was not watched.
  - boneandjoint.gluteus_minimus, Abstract. Qualification: 16 cadaver hips and a plastic-bone model; abstract only.
- `hips.gluteus_minimus.appearance` (appearance, anatomical_inference, reviewed): The gluteus minimus is the smallest and deepest of the gluteal muscles, lying under the gluteus medius on the outer surface of the pelvis, so it has little or no contour of its own; it may add to the general fullness of the side of the hip.
  - radiopaedia.gluteal_muscles, Gluteus minimus: the smallest and deepest. Qualification: Gives location and layer only; the contour statement is inferred from them, not reported by the source.

**Tensor fasciae latae** (`hips.tfl`)

- `hips.tfl.function` (function, established_anatomy, reviewed): The tensor fasciae latae works with the gluteal muscles in bending the hip, moving the thigh out to the side and turning it inward; anatomy references describe it as assisting in each, and one calls it a relatively weak abductor and inward rotator. In walking it helps balance the body's weight over the standing leg, raising the pelvis on the other side so the swinging leg clears the ground. It joins the iliotibial band, which carries its pull to the shin bone below the knee; references disagree about what it does at the knee.
  - statpearls.tensor_fasciae_latae, Structure and Function; Clinical Significance: walking. Qualification: Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
  - elsevier.tensor_fasciae_latae, Quick Facts: Action. Qualification: Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
  - kenhub.tensor_fasciae_latae, Function. Qualification: Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
  - pmid.2621137, Abstract. Qualification: A biomechanical model of the hip abductors; abstract only.
- `hips.tfl.selectivity.1` (selectivity, experimental, reviewed): In a fine-wire EMG study of 20 healthy adults doing 11 exercises, the tensor fasciae latae was less active than both the gluteus medius and the upper gluteus maximus in bridging on one or both legs, hip extension on hands and knees, the clam, side-stepping and squatting. The study measured short-term activity; it does not show that any exercise leaves the tensor fasciae latae out, or which exercise builds which muscle.
  - pmid.23160432, Abstract: Results and Conclusions. Qualification: Short-term muscle activity in a controlled task; not a measurement of growth and not a set-credit weight. Healthy adults; the figures come from secondary summaries of the abstract.
- `hips.tfl.appearance` (appearance, anatomical_inference, reviewed): The tensor fasciae latae is the most superficial muscle at the front and outer side of the top of the thigh, lying just under the skin to the outer side of the hip joint, between two layers of the thigh's fascia; it is easy to feel halfway between the bony point at the front of the pelvis and the bony point at the top of the thigh bone. It may therefore contribute to the contour at the front and outer side of the hip. Visible shape also depends on neighbouring muscles, body fat and individual anatomy.
  - kenhub.tensor_fasciae_latae, Overview; palpation. Qualification: Gives location and layer only; the contour statement is inferred from them, not reported by the source.

**Piriformis** (`hips.piriformis`)

- `hips.piriformis.function` (function, established_anatomy, reviewed): The piriformis is one of the short muscles that turn the thigh outward. With the hip straight it turns the thigh outward, and with the hip bent it helps move the thigh out to the side; it also helps hold the head of the thigh bone in its socket. Its pull changes as the hip bends: by one reference, beyond about 60 degrees of hip flexion it turns the thigh inward instead. That reversal rests mainly on cadaver studies, static imaging and computer models.
  - statpearls.piriformis, Introduction; Structure and Function. Qualification: Introduction and part of Structure and Function only; the rest was behind a registration wall.

## 9. Where sources disagree, and how the wording handles it

- Hip rotation and hip flexion. A cadaver and model study (pmid.10327003) found the pull of most hip muscle parts it measured shifting toward turning the thigh inward as the hip bends, but its abstract does not say which parts. Muscle by muscle the recorded evidence is: piriformis, outward with the hip straight and inward past about 60 degrees by one reference (others, uncited, say about 90), resting mainly on cadaver and model work (wcpt.piriformis_action); gluteus minimus, inward by its front fibres with the hip bent by one reference, either way depending on position and part in a cadaver model; gluteus medius, no muscle-specific evidence found (Q57), so its claim states only the general finding. The claims say this plainly and the node actions count only what holds with the hip straight, as help where the sources say help.
- Adductor longus and hip flexion: a flexor up to about 90 degrees of hip flexion and an extensor beyond in one congress-abstract model (isb.adductor_longus_moment_arm). Said in the claim and a caveat; counted as assist for flexion only.
- Adductor magnus and hip extension: Kenhub and StatPearls give extension to the hamstring part; StatPearls' adductor-strain article says the magnus 'contributes' to extension; an imaging study of 15 young adults estimated its extension capacity at more than twice its adduction capacity. Recorded prime on the first, second and fourth. The claim says what each found.
- Rotation by the adductors: adductor longus outward (Kenhub) versus some inward (StatPearls); adductor brevis outward (Kenhub) versus inward (Radiopaedia), with a Kenhub video calling it contested; pectineus possibly either way (Kenhub); adductor magnus parts listed in opposite directions (Kenhub). None counted; each claim says references disagree.
- Iliopsoas rotation: StatPearls and Kenhub list a small outward part; one study abstract reports the iliacus turning the thigh inward in every position tested. Not counted; the claim says the references do not agree.
- Tensor fasciae latae at the knee: StatPearls an accessory knee flexor beyond 30 degrees with outward turning of the shin; Kenhub knee extension and outward turning. Not counted; the claim and a caveat say references disagree. Its hip actions are 'assists' in StatPearls and Elsevier and 'relatively weak' in Kenhub, which is why all its rows are assist.
- Gluteus medius back fibres: StatPearls ties their outward turning to the knee being straight; Kenhub does not list outward turning at all. The claim says 'one reference adds'.
- Gluteus maximus and buttock shape: Wikipedia (read, not cited) says fat contributes much of the buttock's rounded shape; the shape notes carry the forearm's sentence that visible shape also depends on body fat, and no sentence says the muscle alone makes the outline.
- Psoas minor: absent rates differ widely between studies and populations, and the two cited studies disagree on whether race matters. The variation claim states both without a figure.
- Pectineus nerve supply: Kenhub gives the femoral nerve with the obturator as a variant; Radiopaedia splits the muscle between the femoral and accessory obturator nerves. The claim says 'described as mixed' and 'may supply'.

## 10. Deviations from §1.2

None changes an id, a short name or a role; the adversarial review decides whether either changes a layer or a parent.

**D1. layer: hips.adductor_magnus (intermediate) and hips.adductor_brevis (deep).** Fixed in §1.2: adductor magnus 'intermediate (surface outcrop, back view)'; adductor brevis 'deep'. Evidence: Seen from the front, the adductor brevis lies deep to the pectineus and adductor longus but superficial to the adductor magnus (kenhub.adductor_brevis_video, verbatim: 'the adductor brevis muscle can be found deep to the pectineus and adductor longus muscles but superficial to the adductor magnus muscle'; lecturio.medial_thigh_muscles). §1.2's layers put the magnus above the brevis. Assessment: Not a contradiction for the back view, where the magnus lies under the hamstrings with part of it at the surface and the brevis is not seen, nor for a Front view drawn with only surface and deep layers (§7.2) if the magnus is not drawn there. It is a contradiction if the drawing shows the magnus on the Front at all: anatomically it lies behind the brevis from the front. Proposal: Keep §1.2's ids and layers; the drawing lane does not draw the adductor magnus in the Front view (or draws it on Front-deep only where the brevis does not cover it, which would need a Front-middle layer for the brevis). The adversarial review decides; this lane does not change §1.2.

**D2. parent: hips.tfl (none in §1.2).** Fixed in §1.2: tensor fasciae latae has no parent; the gluteals' children are the gluteus maximus, medius and minimus. Assessment: A grouping choice, not an anatomical error: §1.2 gives the TFL its own body-map key (tfl) and keeps it outside the gluteals. The gluteals' function claim names the TFL as a member of the superficial gluteal group in the references, and the gluteals' caveat says it is listed on its own here. Proposal: No change. Recorded so the review sees the wording choice.
