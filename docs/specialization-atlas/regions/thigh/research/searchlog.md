The StatPearls article "Anatomy, Bony Pelvis and Lower Limb, Hamstring Muscle" is in the results, both at the NCBI Bookshelf and on statpearls.com. The excerpts I could see cover its main structure and function points, though not the full text.

- **Muscles:** The article describes the hamstrings as the biceps femoris (long and short heads), semitendinosus, and semimembranosus. Its table lists each muscle's origin, insertion, function, innervation, and blood supply.
- **Hip extension and knee flexion:** The article says the group contributes strongly to hip extension (moving the femur backward) and knee flexion (moving the tibia and fibula backward). Most of the muscles cross both the hip and knee, which is why they can do both. The short head of the biceps femoris is the exception: it starts on the femur and crosses only the knee. The article notes some authors therefore question whether it is a true hamstring.
- **Gait role:** Per the article, the hamstrings switch on during the final quarter of the swing phase to extend the hip and resist knee extension. They also work with the ACL to resist anterior tibial translation at heel strike.
- **Clinical relevance:** Intact hamstring tendons are strong enough that they are often used as grafts in knee ligament reconstruction.

A separate PMC study (Yanagisawa & Fukutani, 2020) makes the related point that the hamstring muscles differ in architecture, so they should not always be treated as one unit.
