# Specialization atlas: thigh anatomy and its evidence (19 Oct 2026)

What the thigh's anatomy content would say, what it rests on, and how far that review can be trusted. This is the anatomy lane's research dossier for the thigh (wave E, with the hips); the content files it feeds are `shared/specialization/regions/thigh/{nodes,nodeActions,claims}.ts` and `shared/specialization/sources/thigh.ts`. Ids, names, short names, kinds, roles, layers, parents and region actions are those fixed in 10-multi-region.md §1.2; the method, limits and wording follow 03-anatomy-evidence.md. The machine-readable form, with every query and the excerpt relied on for each citation, is `dossier.json` beside this file; the raw search logs are `searchlog.md` (the first pass, Q1 onward) and `searchlog.second-review.md` (the second review, R1 onward). Nothing here reaches the app or the database until the thigh's anatomy lane has turned it into content and that has passed its own adversarial review.

**In short.** 11 nodes, 34 claims (11 function, 11 appearance, 11 selectivity, 1 variation), 31 reviewed and 3 draft; every node reviewed. 14 node actions on the region's four actions (12 prime, 2 assist); the two parents, the quadriceps and the hamstrings, have function claims because the references describe each group as such, and no node actions. No new joint action: all four thigh actions are already in `shared/specialization/actions/joints.ts`, and their wording is copied unchanged. 56 sources, every one cited by a claim; 113 citations, each with its query and excerpt; 3 deviations from §1.2, none of which changes an id or a layer. The head-specific findings the region notes asked for (the rectus femoris with the hip bent or straight, the hamstring heads in hinges against curls) are selectivity claims that say what was measured, in how many people, and that it is not growth. The three draft claims are training-study muscle-size findings (two from one leg-curl study; one, added by the second review, from a leg-extension study of hip angle), held back on principle, as the upper arm held its own (§6), not for want of support. An automated second review on 19 Oct 2026 re-searched 9 of 11 function claims and every selectivity and variation claim, reworded 16 statements, sent no reviewed claim to draft, and changed one node action (the sartorius's knee flexion, now assist) (§13).

## 1. Method

**What was reviewed.** Every node of the thigh in §1.2: the quadriceps (a parent) with the rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, vastus medialis and vastus intermedius; the hamstrings (a parent) with the long and short heads of the biceps femoris, the semitendinosus and the semimembranosus; and the sartorius (adjacent). The external members (the gluteus maximus, adductor magnus and iliopsoas, whose home is the hips, and the gastrocnemius, whose home is the lower leg) belong to their home lanes and are not researched here (§10). For each node, a function and an appearance claim, plus selectivity claims where a citable EMG, imaging or clinical finding exists (8 reviewed, 3 draft) and a variation claim where a real difference between people was found (one).

**How.** Web search only, as for the forearm. Page fetches are refused by the network proxy (10-multi-region.md §8), so each claim was checked against what the search results showed: titles, URLs and the tool's generated summaries with quoted excerpts. 82 searches were logged (Q1 to Q82 in `searchlog.md`), batched by muscle and by question. For each claim:

1. Search for reputable references in the evidence standard's order: NCBI Bookshelf (StatPearls), peer-reviewed papers, university and teaching pages (Loyola University Chicago's gross anatomy dissector and study guide, TeachMeAnatomy), Kenhub, Elsevier Complete Anatomy, Radiopaedia, IMAIOS. Wikipedia, WikiMSK and coaching sites appeared and were read where shown; none is cited.
2. Add each relied-on reference with a `<publisher>.<topic>` key, or `pmid.<number>` where the PubMed record itself was shown, and a note saying exactly what the search showed and what it could not confirm.
3. Cite it on the claim with a locator (the section the excerpt came from) and a qualification where the source is limited (search excerpt or summary, location only, abstract only, acute EMG, muscle imaging after one session, a cadaver leverage study).
4. Record, for every citation, the query and the excerpt relied on, verbatim from the search output (`citations` in the JSON; fragments of one result are joined with ' ... '). The build script refuses an excerpt fragment that is not word for word in `searchlog.md` and a query number that is not logged.
5. Mark a claim `reviewed` only when the recorded excerpts support every sentence, with `reviewedAt: "2026-10-19"` and the note "Checked against the cited sources through web search on 19 Oct 2026; automated review, not a clinician or anatomist." Appearance claims carry the forearm's appearance note adapted to the date and are always `anatomical_inference`.
6. A node is `reviewed` only when all its function claims are. A parent gets a function claim only where the evidence speaks of the group as such (StatPearls' quadriceps, anterior-thigh, hamstring and posterior-thigh articles; Radiopaedia's quadriceps and hamstring articles; Kenhub's and TeachMeAnatomy's group pages), and never a node action.

Wording was rewritten until each sentence said no more than its excerpts. Where references disagree (the rectus femoris and the sartorius at the hip, the hamstrings' strength at the hip, whether the short head of the biceps femoris is a hamstring, whether the vastus medialis's oblique fibres are a separate part), the claim says that they disagree (§6). The build script also checks the copy rules of `regionContent.test.ts` and `forearmAnatomy.test.ts` over every node field, caveat, statement, locator and qualification: no per-cent sign or 'per cent', no growth promise, no 'optimal' or 'guarantee', no vendor name, no 'table', no 'left' or 'right' (directions are front and back, inner and outer, toward the hip or the knee); deep muscles say 'little or no' contour; surface target muscles' shape notes are qualified; every shape note carries the sentence on neighbouring muscles, body fat and individual anatomy; names, short names and aliases are unique and none collides with a hips external's; the four actions' wording equals `joints.ts`. It also checks that no source key or URL is used twice.

**Dates.** The searches ran on 20 Oct 2026; review and retrieval dates are written as 19 Oct 2026, the date fixed for this wave's review. If the lane prefers the run date, every `reviewedAt`, `retrievedOn` and review note changes together.

**This lane ran twice.** This lane's first run on 20 Oct stopped after building dossier.json and before dossier.md. The continuation re-ran the build and its checks, ran two searches (Q81, Q82) that identified the Korean cadaver study, renamed three source keys (see the source-key notes), and tightened one function sentence, six selectivity statements and four caveats to their excerpts: in the rectus femoris function claim the iliopsoas's share 'rises sharply' beyond about 60 degrees, as the abstract says, rather than 'taking over'; the hamstring long head is relatively more active in some hip-extension exercises, not all (a bent-knee bridge gave the lowest ratio); 'only' was dropped from the semimembranosus imaging sentence because the abstract sentence does not say it; the lunge finding names the adductor magnus with the long head; the 36-player imaging study is said to be split among four exercises; the Bourne study is '24 men in all', since the imaging part's own number was not shown; the vasti claims say what the two studies found instead of 'no ordinary exercise is known to train'. The rectus femoris function claim also gained the Radiopaedia line its hip-flexion row already named (Q33). No claim changed status.

## 2. Limits

- **Excerpts and summaries, not full texts.** StatPearls showed introductions and parts of Structure and Function; past that its articles were behind a registration wall. Many results came as generated summaries with a few quoted lines, some drawing on several pages of one publisher at once (TeachMeAnatomy's group page and its encyclopaedia entries; Loyola's two dissector pages for the semimembranosus and semitendinosus). Each citation's qualification says which, and such lines are filed under one key with a note saying so.
- **Search summaries can misquote.** Every function claim rests on four to seven references from four to seven publishers. Lines a summary did not tie to one page were not used (the 'weak' sartorius in Q59, which came from WikiMSK, a user-edited wiki, in any case), and neither were lines from sites outside the evidence standard's list (anatomy.net and anatomy.app on the semimembranosus, Q61). Kenhub's line that the tensor fasciae latae and gluteus maximus cover the vastus lateralis's outer side (Q20) is noted in that node's layer evidence but not cited: read literally it puts two hip muscles over the vastus lateralis, while Kenhub's iliotibial-tract page puts a band of fascia over its lower part (Q57); the review should settle which the visibility rests on. Where a summary's own words went beyond the quoted abstract sentence (the semimembranosus 'only' in Q63), the claim follows the abstract.
- **The head-specific findings rest on abstracts of small studies.** The rectus femoris findings come from two surface-EMG studies, one with 11, 15 and 14 people in its three experiments (found by the second review) and one whose sample size was not shown; the hamstring findings from five studies of 6 to 36 people (EMG, and muscle imaging after one session). Each selectivity claim gives the numbers it could and says the findings are short-term and in small groups.
- **Leverage is not force.** The cadaver study of 22 limbs that estimated the rectus femoris's share of hip-bending force works from moment arms and muscle cross-sections, not measurements in living people; it is cited as an estimate.
- **Automated review.** "Reviewed" here means checked by this automated process against search results. It is not a review by a clinician or an anatomist, and the database lane should not publish it as clinically verified on the strength of it.
- **Nothing reviewed here is hypertrophy evidence.** The reviewed selectivity claims cite acute EMG and imaging studies; they say what was measured and that it is not growth, and they must never become set-credit weights. The two training studies found (seated against lying leg curls; leg extensions at two hip angles, the second found by the second review) are held in three draft claims (§6).

Said plainly, as §8 requires on every region's "How this is counted":

> "Reviewed" means an automated agent checked each sentence against web-search excerpts and generated summaries of the cited sources on the date shown. It was not checked against the full texts, and not by a clinician or anatomist. A second automated review, with the same tools and limits, looked for errors; it is not peer review. The drawings are original schematics checked against these notes, not reviewed by an anatomist.

A second automated review, with the same tools and limits, was run on 19 Oct 2026 (§13). It is not peer review.

### 3.1 The claims as worded

**`thigh.quadriceps.function`** (function, established_anatomy, reviewed 2026-10-19)

> The quadriceps femoris is a group of four muscles at the front of the thigh: the rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, vastus medialis and vastus intermedius. Anatomy references describe it as the main muscle group that straightens the knee, through the quadriceps tendon to the kneecap and on to the top of the shin bone, and as helping keep the kneecap steady. Of the four, only the rectus femoris crosses the hip, where it also helps bend the hip.

- statpearls.anterior_thigh, *Structure and Function: the quadriceps group* (Q4). Seen through a generated web-search summary of the page, not the page itself.
- statpearls.quadriceps, *Function (statpearls.com point-of-care copy)* (Q4). Seen through a generated web-search summary of the page, not the page itself.
- kenhub.quadriceps_femoris, *Function* (Q16). Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
- radiopaedia.quadriceps_muscles, *Gross anatomy: action* (Q33). Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
- teachmeanatomy.anterior_thigh, *Quadriceps femoris and rectus femoris: actions* (Q52). Seen through a generated web-search summary of the page, not the page itself.

**`thigh.quadriceps.appearance`** (appearance, anatomical_inference, reviewed 2026-10-19)

> Together the quadriceps muscles make up most of the front of the thigh: the rectus femoris at the surface in front, the vastus lateralis on the outer side, the vastus medialis on the inner side and the vastus intermedius deep beneath them. So they may shape most of the front of the thigh's contour, mostly through the three at the surface; visible shape also depends on neighbouring muscles, body fat and individual anatomy.

- radiopaedia.quadriceps_muscles, *Gross anatomy: position of the four muscles* (Q33). Gives location and layer only; the contour statement is inferred from them, not reported by the source.
- statpearls.quadriceps, *Introduction* (Q1). Gives location and layer only; the contour statement is inferred from them, not reported by the source.

**`thigh.quadriceps.variation.1`** (variation, established_anatomy, reviewed 2026-10-19)

> The textbook quadriceps has four heads, but many people have more. A 2025 review pooling 18 cadaver studies (1,066 limbs) found a five-headed form in just over half of the limbs and the classic four-headed form in fewer than half; the extra head was most often a head of the vastus lateralis, and an independent tensor of the vastus intermedius was found in a minority. In a cadaver study of 41 limbs from a South Indian population, an extra head lay between the vastus lateralis and the vastus intermedius in a little under half.

- mdpi.quadriceps_morphology_meta, *Abstract: Results* (Q36, R29). Pooled prevalence from 18 cadaver studies; seen through abstract excerpts in two search summaries, not the full text.
- springer.quadriceps_additional_head, *Abstract: Results* (Q65, R11). One cadaver study of 41 limbs from one population; abstract seen through search summaries and a repository copy.

**`thigh.rectus_femoris.function`** (function, established_anatomy, reviewed 2026-10-19)

> The rectus femoris straightens the knee with the other three quadriceps muscles and bends the hip. It is the only quadriceps muscle that crosses the hip: it arises from the front of the pelvis just above the hip joint (the anterior inferior iliac spine and the rim above the hip socket) and joins the other quadriceps muscles in their common tendon (the patellar tendon). References differ on how much it does at the hip. Some say it helps or assists in bending the hip; a cadaver study of 22 limbs estimated from the muscles' size and leverage, as a theoretical share rather than a measured force, that from a straight hip to about 60 degrees of bending it could give roughly two thirds of the hip-bending force, the iliopsoas's share rising sharply beyond that, and counted both among the main hip flexors.

- statpearls.rectus_femoris, *Structure and Function* (Q3). Seen through a generated web-search summary of the page, not the page itself.
- elsevier.rectus_femoris, *Quick Facts: Action; Origin* (Q28). Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
- kenhub.quadriceps_femoris, *Function: rectus femoris* (Q16). Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
- teachmeanatomy.anterior_thigh, *Rectus femoris: attachments and actions* (Q52). Seen through a generated web-search summary of the page, not the page itself.
- loyola.lower_limb_study_guide, *Quadriceps: main actions* (Q15). Seen through a generated web-search summary of the page, not the page itself.
- imaios.rectus_femoris, *Function* (Q53). Seen through a web-search summary of the imaios.cn mirror, not the page.
- radiopaedia.quadriceps_muscles, *Gross anatomy: action* (Q33). Seen through web-search excerpts of the page, not the full text.
- juntendo.hip_flexor_agonists, *Abstract: Results and Conclusion* (Q34, R12). Cadaver morphometry of 22 limbs; the force shares are theoretical estimates from muscle size and leverage, not measurements in living people, as the authors say.

**`thigh.rectus_femoris.selectivity.1`** (selectivity, experimental, reviewed 2026-10-19)

> In a surface-EMG study with three experiments (11, 15 and 14 people), the rectus femoris was more active in knee extension than in a leg press, while the vastus lateralis and vastus medialis did not differ between the two, and adding effort to straighten the hip lowered rectus femoris activity. The authors concluded that its activity in multi-joint exercise is low and does not rise with fatigue, unlike the vasti, and that these differences depend on whether the exercise also asks the hip to straighten. That is short-term muscle activity, not growth.

- springer.quadriceps_single_multi_joint, *Abstract: Methods, Results and Conclusions* (Q37, R1). Short-term muscle activity in a controlled task; not a measurement of growth and not a set-credit weight. Surface electrodes; 11, 15 and 14 people in its three experiments; abstract seen through search summaries.

**`thigh.rectus_femoris.selectivity.2`** (selectivity, experimental, reviewed 2026-10-19)

> In a surface-EMG study of maximal holds, the rectus femoris was less active when bending the hip than when straightening the knee, in each of the three regions recorded along it. When straightening the knee its upper, middle and lower regions did not differ; when bending the hip its lower region was less active than its middle and upper regions. The authors concluded that the muscle is controlled differently along its length. That is short-term muscle activity, not growth.

- plos.rectus_femoris_regional_activity, *Abstract: Results and Conclusions* (Q60, R2). Short-term muscle activity in a controlled task; not a measurement of growth and not a set-credit weight. Maximal isometric efforts; sample size not shown.

**`thigh.rectus_femoris.selectivity.3`** (selectivity, experimental, draft)

> In a 10-week training study of 22 untrained men, each doing leg extensions with the hip bent to about 40 degrees on one side and to about 90 degrees on the other, ultrasound-measured thickness of the rectus femoris rose more on the side trained with the hip less bent, while the vastus lateralis showed no difference between the sides. This is a group result from one study, not a forecast for any one person, and it is not used to weight credit.

**`thigh.sartorius.function`** (function, established_anatomy, reviewed 2026-10-19)

> The sartorius bends the knee and turns the lower leg inward at the knee, and at the hip it helps bend the thigh, move it out to the side and turn it outward, as when sitting cross-legged. ## 6. Single-source claims, disagreements, drafts and what was not claimed

**Single-source claims and sentences.**

- None. Every function claim cites four to seven references.
- Sentences inside function claims that rest on one reference: thigh.rectus_femoris.function, the cadaver estimate of its share of hip-bending force (juntendo.hip_flexor_agonists); thigh.vastus_medialis.function, the review of whether the oblique fibres are a separate part (pmid.19090000) and the oblique fibres as an inner kneecap stabiliser (radiopaedia.vastus_medialis); thigh.vastus_intermedius.function, 'helps steady the kneecap' (teachmeanatomy.anterior_thigh, said as 'one reference adds'); thigh.biceps_femoris_long.function, its hip role as weaker than its knee role (teachmeanatomy.hamstrings, said as 'one teaching reference'); thigh.semimembranosus.function, its pull at the hip as weaker than the biceps femoris's (radiopaedia.semimembranosus, said as 'one reference'); thigh.sartorius.function, the cadaver study's 'synergist' (juntendo.hip_flexor_agonists). Each is worded as one reference's finding. Added by the second review: thigh.sartorius.function, that none of its actions is strong on its own (radiopaedia.sartorius, R16), said as 'the reference that lists its primary actions'.
- Non-function claims resting on one source: thigh.rectus_femoris.selectivity.1 (springer.quadriceps_single_multi_joint), thigh.rectus_femoris.selectivity.2 (plos.rectus_femoris_regional_activity), the two draft training-study claims (pmid.33009197) and the third draft, added by the second review (thigh.rectus_femoris.selectivity.3, pmid.39699974). Every other selectivity claim cites two to four studies; the variation claim cites two.

**Where sources disagree, and how the wording handles it.**

- Rectus femoris at the hip. StatPearls ('helps flex the hip') and Elsevier ('assists') word hip flexion as help; Kenhub, TeachMeAnatomy, Loyola, IMAIOS and Radiopaedia list it as one of its two actions; a cadaver morphometry study estimated it gives roughly two thirds of the hip-flexion torque up to about 60 degrees and named it one of the two main agonists. The claim says the references differ; the node action is prime on the cadaver estimate and the action lists.
- Sartorius at the hip. Radiopaedia lists hip flexion among its primary actions; Elsevier says it assists, and the cadaver study names it a synergist. The claim says the references differ; the node action takes the weaker reading (assist), as the forearm does for helpers.
- Hamstrings at the hip. TeachMeAnatomy calls the biceps femoris's hip role weaker than its knee role, and a Radiopaedia line calls the semimembranosus's hip extension weaker than the biceps femoris's. Elsevier, Kenhub and Loyola list hip extension for each, StatPearls says the group contributes strongly to hip extension, and two EMG and imaging studies found the long head and semimembranosus selectively recruited in hip-extension exercises. Each weaker-role line is said in its claim as one reference's view; the node actions stay prime.
- Short head of the biceps femoris as a hamstring: Elsevier's semitendinosus page names only the three hip-crossing muscles as 'hamstrings' and StatPearls notes some authors question it; StatPearls, Radiopaedia, Kenhub and TeachMeAnatomy list it with the hamstrings. Said in the group claim and the short head's caveats (deviation D3).
- The vastus medialis oblique: Radiopaedia describes the oblique fibres as a component and an inner kneecap stabiliser; a systematic review found too little good-quality evidence to say the muscle divides in two. The function claim says both; the node stays one muscle, as §1.2 has it.
- Hamstring imaging findings conflict for the short head and the semimembranosus: after hip extension their MRI signal rose by different amounts in one study, while another found no significant change in either after leg curls or lunges. The selectivity claims say each finding and call the picture mixed rather than choosing one.
- Rotation at the knee and hip by the hamstrings and the sartorius is position-dependent and secondary in every source; it is said in the claims, never counted (no knee-rotation action exists; hip rotation is not a thigh region action).
- Sartorius at the knee (second review). Every reference lists knee flexion, Elsevier without qualification and Radiopaedia among its primary actions, but Radiopaedia also says none of its actions is strong in isolation and most of its action is synergistic (R16). The claim now says so, and the knee.flexion row is assist, like its hip-flexion row.
- Which head of the biceps femoris is deeper (second review). Kenhub says one head lies deep to the other without naming it, and no excerpt in either pass names it (Q50, R31, R32). The short head as the deeper rests on its origin on the lower back of the thigh bone and on §1.2's layer; the appearance claims now word it as that inference.

**Draft claims.** Three. Two come from one training study (pmid.33009197: 20 untrained adults, seated leg curls with one leg and lying leg curls with the other for 12 weeks, MRI muscle volume): `thigh.biceps_femoris_long.selectivity.2` and `thigh.biceps_femoris_short.selectivity.2`.
