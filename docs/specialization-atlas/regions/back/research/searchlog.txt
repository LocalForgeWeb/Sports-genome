## Q1: StatPearls Anatomy, Back, Trapezius Muscle superior middle inferior fibers elevate retract depress upward rotation scapula

Summary: "The StatPearls "Anatomy, Back, Trapezius" entry matches your query. It describes the upper fibers as the ones that elevate and upwardly rotate the scapula, the middle fibers as adductors that medially retract it, and the lower fibers as depressors that help the upper fibers rotate the scapula upward (1-7, 1-8, 1-9). It also notes that the scapula's rotation against the levator scapulae and rhomboids, together with the deltoid, is essential for throwing." "Other references describe the same three regions with slightly different wording. Kenhub says the superior fibers draw the scapula superomedially, the middle fibers draw it medially, and the inferior fibers draw it inferomedially (5-1). Anatomy.app says the ascending (inferior) part medially rotates and depresses the scapula (8-4). The main difference is that StatPearls frames the lower fibers mainly as depressors that assist upward rotation, while Anatomy.app and Kenhub emphasize medial rotation or inferomedial pull."

Summary: "Descending (upper) fibers: These work with levator scapulae to elevate the scapula, and they help hold up the shoulders against gravity when carrying a load (3-6, 3-7). Their unilateral action also laterally flexes the head and neck (3-9)." "Transverse (middle) fibers: These pull the scapula toward the midline and retract it, working with the rhomboids (3-1)." "Ascending (lower) fibers: Their more vertical line of pull makes them effective for depression and upward rotation, with only assistance in retraction (5-5)." The upper fibers are often credited with upward rotation, which Kenhub's trapezius page attributes to the trapezius working with serratus anterior (3-2)." "The conflict: one Kenhub overview assigns elevation and depression to the lower fibers and upper/middle fibers to extension, elevation, and retraction (4-7, 4-8, 4-9). ## Q6: "Anatomy, Back, Trapezius" StatPearls NCBI Bookshelf NBK
Links: ncbi NBK518994; ncbi NBK537216; livewell.uky.edu; statpearls point-of-care/30528; primarycarenotebook; anatomy.app; anatomy.net
Summary: "The StatPearls article "Anatomy, Back, Trapezius" is NCBI Bookshelf entry NBK518994. Its overview describes the trapezius as a broad, superficial back muscle that spans from the skull and spine out to the shoulder blade. Its nerve supply is split between sensory fibers from the cervical rami and motor control from the spinal accessory nerve. Its main job is to move and steady the scapula. The upper fibers elevate and rotate it upward, the middle fibers pull it back toward the spine, and the lower fibers pull it down. The article also notes that the muscle mainly supports posture." "Related StatPearls entry: NBK537216 covers the back muscles more broadly and lists the trapezius among the extrinsic back muscles." "The StatPearls copies I saw were dated 2023 (statpearls.com) and 2026 (ncbi.nlm.nih.gov)."
