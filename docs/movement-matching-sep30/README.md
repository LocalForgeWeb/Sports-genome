## 1. Root cause of the Body Lab handoff

Locations below are at the before commit, 47a528e.

1. **The button did muscle browsing.** The Body Lab's next-step button (`client/src/pages/Home.tsx:1894`) was:
   ```ts
   const target = activeMuscle || getMovementMuscles(referenceMovement)[0];
   // onClick:
   setCatalogFilters({ ...defaultCatalogFilters, muscle: target }); navigateWorkspace("catalog");
   ```
   With no muscle tapped, it used the movement's "first" muscle, labelled the button "Find {muscle} exercises", and opened the plain catalog with a muscle filter. Nothing about the movement went with it.
2. **The "first" muscle was an accident of ordering.**
   - `getMovementMuscles` (`client/src/lib/movementRecommendations.ts:179-181`) returns every key of the `humanMuscleAliases` dictionary (`:72-96`) whose alias appears anywhere in the movement's legacy text. It returns them in the dictionary's order, not by importance.
   - Bridge's stabilizers text is "neck musculature, trunk, **shoulders**, feet/ankles" (`client/src/lib/sportMovementDatabase.ts:556`).
   - `shoulders` comes before `glutes` in the dictionary, so Bridge led to "Find shoulders exercises" and a `muscle=shoulders` filter.
3. **The catalog listed the filter's results in catalog order.** That gave 19 exercises in id order, led by Cable Wood Chop (id 250) and Landmine Rotation (id 265). Their gold "S" was the catalog's static `muscleGrade`, which says nothing about Bridge.
4. **The context line told a different story.**
   - "Action links below are measured against **bridge**. All 19 share a muscle demand with it." (`client/src/components/CatalogDiscoveryPanel.tsx:68,103`).
   - The line was bound to the profile's movement and to a list-level summary, not to how the list was built.
   - So a shoulder-filtered list appeared under Bridge's name.
5. **A tapped muscle carried over.** A muscle tapped earlier stayed in `activeMuscle`, and `inspectExercise` also wrote to it. Either could quietly decide the next "Find" result. That is the stale selection the brief asked about.

## 2. How candidates are selected, and missing data

The one source is the movement's enriched research record (`client/src/lib/enrichedSportMovementDatabase.ts`), keyed by sport and movement id. The logic is in `client/src/lib/movementSupport.ts`.

| Tier | Rule | Counted as a match |
|---|---|---|
| **Movement-specific** | The exercise name contains, as whole words, an exercise the record names in `recommendedExercises`. "Barbell Hip Thrust" contains "hip thrust". 7 exact same-exercise synonyms and a short "not the same exercise" list cover known misreads. | Yes |
| **Related pattern** | Shares a catalog movement pattern with a movement-specific exercise, and has one of the record's prime movers as a primary muscle. Patterns used by more than 15 exercises (`BROAD_PATTERNS`) do not count. | Yes |
| **Muscle support** | Has one of the record's prime movers as a primary muscle, and nothing more. | No. It sits in a collapsed, separately labelled section. |

What is not used to place an exercise:
- the catalog's `qualities`;
- the Matches engine's text signals;
- the catalog's `muscleGrade` (S/A) and `sportFit`;
- the sport-level research registry.

The "mechanical match" signal in `exerciseGenome.ts` disagreed with the tiers. It scored Barbell Hip Thrust 35 and Landmine Rotation 48 for Bridge. It was removed as an input to the contextual grade.

Every movement result has a "Why it appears" line built from the data, for example "Named in the Bridge movement record: hip thrust". Exercise details show the tier and how it was matched. The S badge is gone from discovery rows. Where it remains, in details, it reads "Catalog tier S", with a note that it is a general catalog label and not a movement match.

**Missing data.** `getMovementSupport` returns a status, and each status has its own screen:

| Status | Count (of 425 movements) | What the athlete sees |
|---|---|---|
| `ok` | 386 | The tiers above. |
| `no-record` | 25 | "Movement-specific matches aren't available yet". Actions: browse the movement's muscles, or show all exercises. |
| `no-named-matches` | 14 | The same message, plus the record's named exercises that the catalog does not hold, and the same actions. |
| Filters leave zero results | n/a | "No matches with these filters". Clear filters keeps the movement. |

No result is filled in or invented when the data is missing.

## 3. State and navigation changes

**Discovery context**
- The discovery context is explicit and separate from the add destination (`client/src/lib/exerciseDiscovery.ts`):
  ```ts
  type ExerciseDiscoveryContext =
    | { mode: "movement"; sportId: string; movementId: string }
    | { mode: "muscle"; muscleId: string }
    | { mode: "all" };
  ```
- The context lives in the URL, using stable ids:
  - `?workspace=catalog&discover=movement&sport=wrestling&movement=wrestling-19`
  - `&discover=muscle&muscle=glutes`
- Invalid or unknown values fall back to the whole catalog. A mixed context is never shown.

**Entry points**
- The single entry point is `openDiscovery` (`Home.tsx:1097`).
- **Find exercises for {Movement}** sits on the Movement explorer and the Body Lab. It is always movement mode, whatever muscle is tapped. The `getMovementMuscles` fallback is gone.
- **Browse {Muscle} exercises** is a separate action. It appears only after a muscle has been picked.
- `inspectExercise` no longer writes the active muscle.

**Results header**
- Movement mode shows "Exercises for Bridge", then "Wrestling · Bridge", "Back to Bridge", "Show all exercises", and a "How matches work" disclosure.
- The count reads "27 movement matches" (15 movement-specific · 12 related pattern). Muscle support is never included in it.

**Back and history**
- A fresh entry clears refinements, starts at the top, and adds a history entry. Back returns to the movement and the page it was opened from.
- Opening an exercise and returning keeps the context, the refinements and the scroll position.
- Results are computed synchronously from the context key, so an older movement's results cannot show under a newer heading.

**Filters that are kept on purpose**
- Equipment and other refinements work inside movement mode, as visible, removable chips. They never displace the "Wrestling · Bridge" line.
- **Clear filters** clears refinements and keeps the movement.
- Each context keeps its own refinements for Back and Forward. A fresh entry never inherits them.
- The add destination ("Adding to Week 1 · Push") is Plan's open day. Discovery does not change it, and Home's next workout does not follow it.

**Strength, analysis and layout**
- **Strength detail:**
  - On a phone the record is a modal sheet: dialog role, scrim, an inert background including the dock, a focus trap, and focus and scroll restored on close.
  - The header stays fixed (title, lift and date, Close, muscle-group select). The body is a single scroll region, and "Open Plan" sits in the footer.
  - The region rank lists the exercises and dated lifts it came from, with each one's share when there is more than one.
  - The lift section separates "Comparison rank for this lift" from "Your progress on this lift". Progress shows the "Estimated 1RM change" with dates and the number of logs.
  - Ranks that are reloading keep the previous values with "Updating ranks…".
- **Analysis:** the page has three named sections: Coverage, Workload and Muscle breakdown.
- **Layout:**
  - The Week loaded toast is gone. The selected week pill is the feedback.
  - The profile prompt is moved below the day's actions.
  - One phone gutter token, `--sg-gutter`, is used.
  - lucide icons replace the text glyphs.
  - The add-destination strip never cuts the day name.

The analysis "100%" (journey 16) traced to relative involvement:
- It is each muscle's sum of role weights times targeting scores over the day's exercises, as a share of the day's highest muscle. Set counts are not used.
- Anterior deltoid read 100% because 17 catalog rows list a muscle as both primary and secondary. Every overhead press does this for front delts, so they were counted twice.
- Fix 1: the muscle now counts once, as a prime mover.
- Fix 2: the line names its basis, for example "Highest relative involvement in this day" or "77% of Triceps brachii's involvement, the day's highest".
- Fix 3: the bars print direct and supporting sets separately on one scale.
