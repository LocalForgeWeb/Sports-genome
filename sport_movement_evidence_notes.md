# Sport Movement Expansion: Evidence Rules

The expanded database contains **400 movement records across 20 sports**, with 20 records per sport. Each record records phase-aware prime movers, assisting muscles, stabilizers, joint actions, contraction roles, exercise-pattern rationale, cautions, source links, and an evidence-confidence label. The detailed wrestling set covers penetration entries, single- and double-leg attacks, hand-fighting, sprawls, turns, bridges, mat returns, lifts, and grip-based control.

The recommendation layer treats resistance exercises as **mechanical preparation**, not a substitute for technical practice or a guarantee of sport-performance improvement. A movement recommendation requires matching body actions, force direction, joint actions, muscle roles, and programming context. It must disclose caution language and confidence, particularly where evidence supports movement mechanics but does not test a direct exercise-to-performance outcome.

The redundancy model will flag similarity across four dimensions: primary movement pattern, shared target muscles, force/skill demand, and fatigue/recovery cost. It will not classify two exercises as low value merely because they share one muscle; overlap may be deliberate when volume, range, skill, force vector, or sport-movement demand differs.

The recovery layer treats volume, proximity to failure, lower-body multi-joint work, eccentric emphasis, lengthened loading, and technical demand as distinct recovery considerations rather than using a single fatigue score. This follows the review’s conclusion that recovery demands depend on exercise selection and how training variables are arranged across the microcycle.[1]
