# Detailed Muscle Atlas Research Notes

The clickable surface atlas will use **anatomical subdivisions** and will not imply that every visible overlay is directly palpable, independently isolateable, or a direct EMG result. The deltoid will be presented as anterior/clavicular, middle/acromial, and posterior/spinal subdivisions. A Journal of Anatomy study notes that these are the classical three portions and reports deeper functional segmentation; the interactive map will show the classical three, which are more appropriate as stable, user-facing surface regions than an over-precise seven-segment visual claim. [1]

For the thigh, the atlas will distinguish the anterior, medial, and posterior compartments. The quadriceps record will separately label rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, and vastus medialis; vastus intermedius will be marked as a deep underlying contributor. The source confirms the four quadriceps muscles share knee-extension function, while rectus femoris also assists hip flexion. It also records that sartorius, iliacus, psoas major, and pectineus contribute to hip-flexor anatomy and that pectineus additionally adducts the thigh. [2]

The model will display numeric values only as standardized, transparent planning estimates, never as a claim of a direct activation measurement. Where the product discusses muscle actions, it will label the source class as **established anatomy**. Where it links exercises to regional emphasis, sport transfer, or program selection, it will label the logic as **context-sensitive training inference**.
