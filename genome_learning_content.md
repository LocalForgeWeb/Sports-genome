# Exercise Genome Learning Content

The **Exercise Genome** scores are standardized planning estimates that help compare exercise attributes. They are not direct measurements of individual muscle activation, readiness, or outcome. An exercise’s practical value changes with the athlete, technique, loading, range of motion, equipment, training age, and current program.

| Dimension | Plain-language meaning | What the estimate reflects |
|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy | How well the exercise can support muscular size work. | Target-muscle loading potential, usable range of motion, controllable resistance, and ability to accumulate productive sets. |
| Strength | How suitable the exercise is for developing high force. | External load potential, compound force expression, bracing demands, and progression potential. |
| Power | How suitable the exercise is for fast force expression. | Intent to move quickly, explosive coordination, and freedom to accelerate through the action. |
| Stability | How much the exercise challenges positional control. | Balance, unilateral control, bracing, external instability, and joint-position demands. |
| Mobility | How much usable movement range the exercise asks for. | Joint excursion, end-range tolerance, and the positions required to perform the movement well. |
| Skill | How much practice and coordination the movement usually requires. | Timing, sequencing, technical constraints, and error sensitivity. |
| Practicality | How straightforward the exercise is to use in a real plan. | Equipment access, setup time, space, load changes, and predictable progression. |

The fingerprint graphic should show the full name for each dimension at its spoke and a visible 0–100 comparison scale. Selecting a spoke or the matching score tile opens the same explanation panel, including a short definition, the inputs considered, an interpretation sentence, and the evidence boundary.
