DO NOT CONFUSE EVIDENCE TYPES

Examples:

A cross-sectional study showing:

high horizontal force ↔ faster acceleration

is:

ASSOCIATIONAL / BIOMECHANICAL

It does NOT prove:

training horizontal force will necessarily cause the exact predicted increase in sprint speed.

A randomized resisted-sprint intervention showing improved 10-m performance is:

LONGITUDINAL CAUSAL EVIDENCE

and should receive greater weight for prescribing resisted sprint training.

==================================================
ACCELERATION VS MAX VELOCITY

Do NOT assume identical training optimizes both.

Acceleration tends to emphasize:

horizontal impulse
horizontal force orientation
longer contact times relative to max velocity
large propulsive impulses
high acceleration-specific power
hip/knee extension
initial projection mechanics

Maximum velocity places greater emphasis on:

very short force-production windows
high-speed force capability
vertical support
elastic behavior
rapid limb repositioning
hamstring mechanics
hip torque
high RFD

==================================================
RESISTED SPRINT LOGIC

Do not label:

resisted sprint = universally superior

Evidence is more nuanced.

Resisted sprinting appears particularly useful for:

initial acceleration
horizontal force capability
acceleration-specific mechanical overload

but unresisted sprinting remains essential.

Store sled loading using:

sled_mass
percent_body_mass
velocity_decrement
surface
distance
repetitions
sets
rest
athlete_level

Whenever possible prefer:

velocity_decrement

to assuming the same % body mass creates the same resistance between athletes/surfaces.

INTENT TO MOVE FAST

Actual bar velocity and intended movement velocity are different.

Heavy resistance can move slowly despite maximal explosive intent.

Therefore store:

and:

intended_concentric_velocity

separately.

For RFD/power work, explosive intent may matter even when external load limits actual movement velocity.

COD ANGLE LOGIC

A 45-degree cut and 180-degree turn are mechanically different.

As direction-change angle increases, generally increase weighting toward:

braking
eccentric_strength
deceleration
reorientation
reacceleration

Do not apply one generic COD recommendation across all tasks.

==================================================
AGILITY ≠ CHANGE OF DIRECTION

Define:

COD =
preplanned physical direction change
AGILITY =
perception
+ decision
+ reaction
+ physical direction change

Gym training can influence physical COD components.

It cannot completely reproduce perceptual/reactive sport skill.

==================================================
RSA TRAINING LOGIC

Repeated sprint performance depends on both:

single-sprint ability

and:

ability to reproduce sprint performance

These are related but not identical.

Therefore a program can improve RSA through:

faster underlying sprint speed
greater mechanical efficiency
better aerobic recovery
better phosphocreatine recovery
greater fatigue resistance

Do not assume every RSA improvement means better metabolic recovery.

Do not reason:

exercise visually resembles sport movement
= optimal transfer

Example:

A heavy squat looks little like sprinting but may improve underlying force capacity.

A resisted sprint has much greater movement similarity but cannot replace general maximal-strength development in every athlete.

CAPACITY DEVELOPMENT

EXAMPLE: FOOTBALL RECEIVER

Athlete:

high maximal squat strength
poor 0–10 m acceleration
good maximum velocity
poor broad jump
average CMJ

Possible hypothesis:

maximal strength is not the obvious primary bottleneck
possible limitations:
horizontal ballistic ability
horizontal impulse
acceleration technique
early acceleration force orientation

Possible priorities:

short acceleration sprints
resisted sprints
broad jumps
bounds
ballistic lower-body work

Maintain strength rather than adding unnecessary heavy squat volume.

EVIDENCE WEIGHTING

Use these only as initial heuristic weights:

Meta-analysis of direct interventions           0.95
High-quality systematic review                  0.90
Randomized longitudinal intervention            0.90
Direct sport-performance intervention           0.95
Direct biomechanics                             0.85
Validated kinetics / force-platform study       0.85
Musculoskeletal simulation                      0.70
Cross-sectional athlete association             0.60
Acute physiological study                       0.55
EMG-only study                                  0.40
Theoretical perspective                         0.35
Expert inference                                0.25

Then modify by:

directness
population_similarity
sport_similarity
training_status_similarity
sample_size
methodological_quality
measurement_validity
recency
replication

==================================================
IMPORTANT: META-ANALYSIS IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY KING

A broad meta-analysis may have high evidence quality but low directness.

Example:

Question:

Does heavy sled sprinting improve 0–10 m acceleration?

A direct resisted-sprint meta-analysis receives high weight.

Question:

What does the biceps femoris do during maximal sprinting?

A high-quality direct sprint biomechanics study may receive greater useful weight than a general training meta-analysis.
