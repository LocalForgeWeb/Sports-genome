# Exercise Genome Evidence Notes

The Exercise Genome is a **planning-comparison model**, not an exercise prescription engine, clinical assessment, injury screen, or direct measurement of force, muscle activation, or individual adaptation.

## Mobility and range-of-motion context

Alizadeh et al. reported in a systematic review and meta-analysis that resistance training with external loads can improve range of motion and that, across the included studies, its range-of-motion effects did not differ significantly from stretching. This supports treating the model’s mobility value as a description of positional and range-of-motion demand—not a flexibility diagnosis or a claim that every exercise improves mobility equally.[1]

## Serratus anterior mechanics

Neumann and Camargo describe serratus anterior’s important roles in scapular protraction and upward rotation, and discuss targeted exercise selection in the context of shoulder-complex kinesiology. This supports tagging scapular-protraction work as serratus-relevant while retaining the app’s explicit boundaries around individual technique, loading, and symptoms.[2]
