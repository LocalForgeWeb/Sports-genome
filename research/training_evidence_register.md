# Training Evidence Register

## Scope and Interpretation

This register distinguishes **source-supported prescription anchors** from app-level planning defaults. Exercise catalog involvement, fatigue, stack coverage, and equivalent-set values are model estimates; they are not electromyography, force-plate, or individual-response measurements.

| Domain | Source-supported finding | App use | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| General resistance training | Regular participation and individualization outweigh rigid, one-size-fits-all protocols. | Keep goal ranges as adaptable planning defaults rather than universal targets. | The ACSM summary addresses healthy adults; athletes may need sport-specific programming. |
| Strength | The 2026 ACSM summary identifies heavier loading around 80% 1RM with 2–3 sets per exercise as a strength-oriented anchor. | Strength primary-lift cue can reference heavy, quality-focused work rather than a fixed number of high-fatigue sets. | The app does not assess 1RM, readiness, coaching, or technique. |
| Hypertrophy volume | The 2026 ACSM summary presents approximately 10 weekly sets per muscle group as a practical reference. | Use approximately 10 as a **starting reference**, not a pass/fail threshold. | Counting differs by exercise, muscle role, effort, experience, and recovery. |
| Power | The 2026 ACSM summary identifies 30–70% 1RM with fast concentric intent as a power-oriented anchor. | Preserve crisp-effort and speed-loss cautions for athleticism/power work. | The app does not measure bar velocity or actual loading. |
| Rest and hypertrophy | A 2024 systematic review found substantial uncertainty across rest durations, with a possible small benefit to resting more than 60 seconds and no clear hypertrophy difference beyond 90 seconds. | Remove the implication that 60 seconds is inherently optimal; allow more rest when it preserves useful performance and volume. | Rest choice is exercise-, load-, and athlete-dependent. |
| Multi-set strength/power | The 2009 ACSM position stand reported that 3–5 minutes can support repeated high-force/power sets and recommended 1–6 RM emphasis for advanced strength work; it also stated that recommendations depend on goals, capacity, and training status. | Retain longer-rest prompts for demanding primary work while explicitly framing them as planning ranges. | Older guidance is contextualized by the 2026 overview rather than treated as a single mandatory protocol. |
