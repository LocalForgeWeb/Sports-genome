## Priority source anchors

Sprint acceleration should be represented as an interaction among sprint-phase mechanics rather than a fixed strength score. The verified sprint-mechanics records cover horizontal force orientation, propulsive/braking impulse, body position, hamstring and lower-limb mechanics, joint moments, and force–velocity relationships. Relevant PMIDs are **26733889, 22422028, 21364480, 25640466, 19863962, 16131703, 21912301, 18317373, 36433206, 36151260, 31340080, and 35903404**. The last three categories include a measurement comparison, predictive simulation, and theoretical perspective, respectively; they must remain labeled as such.

Resisted sprint evidence supports a **probabilistic acceleration-oriented modality**, not an equipment mandate. The verified review and intervention records are **26553497, 29926369, 41384249, 39392558, 39253367, 40325684, 17313259, 16230978, 35510888, 40961280, 24736770, 41327366, 30289907, 31742795, and 37536676**. Sled towing, pushing, vests, assisted sprinting, and other resistance methods remain distinct exposures. The model must not infer a single optimal percentage body-mass load or general maximum-speed transfer.

Force–velocity and jump-profile work is suitable for repeated monitoring only when collection conditions remain comparable. The packet verifies **30273283, 24227123, 34775654, 36662027, 31091259, 40593873, 41766810, 39604155, 32329976, and 36924471**. The 2025 evidence base and methodological critique are explicit counterweights against presenting an inferred imbalance as a deterministic prescription.

RFD is a protocol-dependent performance descriptor shaped by neural drive, contractile properties, musculotendinous behavior, test instructions, time window, equipment, and analysis procedures. Verified records are **26941023, 29266685, 32034703, 12235031, 25754620, 36123735, 27744584, 17219170, 29196141, 26049793, 33217725, 42094307, 26433597, and 41167183**. It should not be converted into a universal load, time-window, or sprint-transfer target.

## Implementation rules

1. **Profile as monitor, not prescription.** Force–velocity, RFD, RSI, CMJ, and RSA values can describe repeated task-specific trends only if testing methods are stable.
2. **Separate outcomes.** Acceleration, maximum velocity, jump, COD, agility, mean/best repeated-sprint output, sprint decrement, and match transfer remain different outcomes.
3. **Keep movement and method distinct.** Sled tow, sled push, weighted vest, assisted sprinting, vertical/horizontal plyometrics, unilateral/bilateral jumps, and reactive agility are not interchangeable labels.
4. **Preserve uncertainty.** Simulations, theory, acute mechanics, and group-average results can inform hypotheses, not athlete-specific causal recommendations.
5. **Use counterevidence visibly.** Individualized F–V profiling, passive stiffness, RSI, CMJ monitoring, and resisted sprinting all require constraints against universal rankings or diagnosis.
