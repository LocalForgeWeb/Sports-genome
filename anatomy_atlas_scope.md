# Detailed Anatomy Atlas Scope

The atlas will model visible, training-relevant skeletal-muscle regions as **named subdivisions** rather than generic zones. The research queue is organized into eight independent systems: chest; shoulder and scapular musculature; upper arm; forearm and hand; trunk and spinal stabilizers; back; hip and pelvis; thigh; and lower leg/foot. Each system will be researched and modeled with anterior/posterior surface placement, a full anatomical label, primary biomechanical actions, functional role, sport-movement relevance, supporting exercise links, and an evidence-confidence descriptor.

The visual model will separate the **anterior, middle, and posterior deltoid**; clavicular and sternal portions of pectoralis major; triceps heads; biceps brachii and brachialis; rectus abdominis and internal/external obliques; latissimus dorsi, trapezius regions, rhomboids, and erector spinae; gluteal regions; quadriceps components; hamstring components; adductor and abductor groups; tibialis anterior; and calf complexes. Regions that cannot be represented reliably as a distinct, click-sized surface will be grouped transparently and labeled as a group rather than falsely isolated.

## Evidence Standard

The atlas will distinguish **anatomical action** from **exercise-specific activation**. Anatomical action statements will cite authoritative anatomy or biomechanics references. Exercise-to-muscle connections will be expressed as either a qualitative role, a standardized model estimate, or a direct-study finding where a comparable measurement is available. No interface score will be presented as an individually validated EMG or outcome measurement unless a source directly supports that exact exercise, task, loading, technique, and population.
